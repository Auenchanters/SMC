Soil-moisture survey: 3D replay

A small robot drives between rows of tomato plants without touching them. At each stop it pushes a probe into the soil and measures how much water is there.

From a few dozen measurements it draws a moisture map of the whole field, so a farmer can see which parts are too dry.

How to read this

The robot
A 4-wheel rover about 50 cm long. It only drives along the paths between plant rows and turns around at the ends of the field, so it never touches a plant. The tall tower on top raises and lowers the soil probe.
A stop
The robot parks, lowers the probe, pushes its three metal rods 6 cm into the soil, measures for a moment, pulls it out through brushes that wipe it clean, blows it dry with air, and checks it is clean before driving on.
Round marks
Every place the robot measured gets a round mark on the ground, coloured by the moisture it measured. Point at a mark to see its value. A grey mark means the probe failed at that spot.
Moisture, in %
How much of the soil's volume is water. 25% means a quarter of the soil volume is water. Below counts as too dry here.
Robot's map
The robot's best guess for every spot in the field, filled in between its marks. It gets better with every reading.
Real moisture
The true moisture, which exists only because this is a simulation. The robot never sees it. Compare the two to judge the map.
Map error
How far the robot's map is from the truth at each spot. "Map is off by" is the average of that, in % points.
Uncertainty
How unsure the robot is about each spot: high far from any mark, low near one. Smart strategies drive to where it is high.
Plants touched
How many times the robot's body overlapped a plant's leaves. The goal is always 0.

Controls

Mouse: drag to turn the view, right-drag to slide it, scroll to zoom. Keys: Space play or pause, ← → previous or next stop, 1–5 ground colours, F follow the robot, O overhead view.

The soil and probe numbers are placeholders until they are calibrated with real field data, so treat the values as an example, not a measurement.
